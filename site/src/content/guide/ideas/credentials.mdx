A credential is a promise you can check. It names a skill, says what its holder can do, and comes with a test that proves it. If you can't check it, it isn't a credential yet.

The app draws each credential as a badge. Badge and credential mean the same thing: the screens say badge, and your folder and exports say credential.

## Its parts

Open a badge from home to see its parts. Each one opens on its own card when you want to change it, as [Change a badge](/how-to/change-a-badge/) shows.

- <UI>Name</UI>: what the skill is called.
- <UI>Learning targets</UI>: what its holder can do, one line each.
- <UI>Task</UI>: what an agent is asked to do in a test.
- <UI>Rubric</UI>: how you judge what comes back.
- <UI>Look</UI>: the badge's colors, art and title.

## Learning targets

A learning target says one thing the holder can do, in the form "I can …". For example, *I can read a repo's code, docs and history and say what it is for.*

Keep a target to the skill itself. How you'll check it belongs in the rubric. When a target carries a list, a number or a "by doing this" clause, the app offers <UI>Move to the rubric</UI>. When a target joins two skills, it asks <UI>Two skills, or one workflow?</UI>

## The task

The task is the test itself: what to do, where, and what to hand back. Write it so an agent, or a person, could do it with nothing else in front of them. A task can hold a [picture](/how-to/paste-an-image/) or a [video](/how-to/add-a-video/), but the handoff carries only the file's name, so say in words what the agent needs from it.

When you hand a task to an agent, the app copies it inside a handoff, a prompt that says this is a test and carries the badge's name, learning targets and task. The handoff never carries the rubric. [Hand off a test](/how-to/hand-off-a-test/) follows one from start to finish.

## The rubric

The rubric is yours. It lists what a good result has, and ends with when it counts as met, such as "Met when every point holds." Write it before you read any reply, so a confident answer can't move the bar.

## Where to start

<UI>Create a credential</UI> on home offers presets: credentials with learning targets, a task and a rubric already written, such as *Say how sure you are* and *Check an answer key*. Or choose <UI>Build your own</UI> and write it from your own work. [Build a credential](/how-to/build-a-credential/) walks through both.

A badge isn't tied to one agent. Any of your agents can take its test, and any of them can hold it.
