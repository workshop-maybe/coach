Jev Mode connects the app to Jev, TypeSafe's model, with your own TypeSafe key. It is off by default, and it is the app's only network connection. Jev acts only when you ask, in two places: a second opinion on a reply, and a cleanup of a reply pasted from a terminal.

## Turn it on

<Shot id="jev-off" alt="The Jev Mode card with Jev Mode off: the Your TypeSafe key field, what each use sends, and the Turn on Jev Mode button" />

1. On home, click <UI>Jev Mode</UI> in the top bar.
2. Paste your key into the field under <UI>Your TypeSafe key</UI>.
3. Click <UI>Turn on Jev Mode</UI>.

Turning it on sends nothing. The app keeps the key in your system's keychain and never shows it again, only its last four characters. If your system has no keychain, as on some Linux setups, the app says so and holds the key only until you quit.

## What each use sends

Nothing is sent until you ask.

- **A second opinion** sends the reply, the badge's task, rubric and learning targets, and the openings of a few earlier replies on this badge with your notes on them.
- **A cleanup** sends only the text you pasted.

The earlier replies come from any of your agents, and only from the badge you're judging. Each is cut to its first few lines, without pictures or videos, and lines that look like keys or passwords are left out. Nothing else from your folder goes with either one. TypeSafe keeps what it receives under [its terms](https://docs.typesafe.ai/legal).

## A second opinion

When Jev Mode is on and the badge has a rubric, <UI>Did it meet it</UI> changes in two ways:

- <UI>Met</UI>, <UI>Not yet</UI> and <UI>Hold to compare</UI> become choices, and <UI>Save</UI> records the one you pick.
- <UI>Ask Jev</UI> appears under the rubric. Once you've picked, it reads <UI>Check with Jev</UI>.

<Shot id="jev-ask" alt="Did it meet it with Jev Mode on: the badge's rubric with Ask Jev under it, the reply beside it, the Met, Not yet and Hold to compare buttons, and Save, greyed out until you pick" />

When the badge has earlier replies, a line under <UI>Ask Jev</UI> says how many go with this one: <UI>Also sends \{n\} earlier replies on this badge</UI>, ending <UI>with your notes</UI> when any of your notes go too.

<Shot id="jev-sends" alt="The rubric side of Did it meet it on Say how sure you are, with Ask Jev under the rubric and the line Also sends 2 earlier replies on this badge with your notes" />

Jev sees them as examples of your own verdicts: a reply you judged Met, and a reply that missed a point you tapped. Replies where you went against Jev's call, and your notes on why, go first. When your verdict goes against Jev's call on the whole bar, one more step asks <UI>What did Jev miss</UI>, and the line you write becomes that note: see [Judge a reply](/how-to/judge-a-reply/#a-second-opinion).

Jev's reading shows below the rubric as calls. Each rubric point, the <UI>Overall</UI> bar and each of the badge's learning targets reads <UI>Meets</UI>, <UI>Misses</UI> or <UI>Can't call</UI>, with Jev's chance beside it in small type. A point Jev can't call either way says <UI>Can't call: sharpen this point</UI>. You still make the call, and the reading is saved with your result. If you hold the reply to compare instead, the reading stays with it and goes into the result when you judge it.

### How a call is read

A new badge reads anything above 70% as meets, anything below 30% as misses, and everything from 30% to 70% as can't call. Once a badge has eight results judged with Jev's reading, at least two of them Met and two Not met, the app fits those cut-offs to your verdicts there. When that moves them, the reading says <UI>Cut-offs fitted to your verdicts</UI>. Rubric points and learning targets use the same cut-offs as the whole bar, since only the whole bar has your verdict on every result. Moving the cut-offs re-reads past readings without asking Jev again.

Once the badge has eight judged readings, and after you pick <UI>Met</UI> or <UI>Not yet</UI>, the reading also shows how often Jev's call on this badge has matched yours, such as <UI>Jev matched your verdict \{k\} of \{c\} times on this badge</UI>. It waits for your pick so it can't lean on your verdict.

To see a reading again, open the reply from the badge's <UI>Replies</UI>, as [Compare takes](/how-to/compare-takes/) shows. The reading is folded under the reply, with its overall call on the fold. Click the fold to open it.

<Shot id="jev-reading" alt="Codex's reply on Check an answer key, with Jev's reading open below it: each rubric point called Meets with its chance beside it, Meets overall at 91%, and the two learning targets, one Meets and one Can't call" />

A reply too long for Jev shows <UI>This reply is too long for Jev</UI>, and you judge it as usual.

## A cleanup

A reply copied out of a terminal often breaks lines where the terminal wrapped them. When a paste looks like that, <UI>Clean up with Jev</UI> appears under the reply box.

<Shot id="jev-cleanup" alt="What came back with a reply pasted from a terminal, its sentences broken where the terminal wrapped them, and Clean up with Jev under the reply box" />

Jev finds the breaks that are only wraps and joins those lines. Nothing else in the reply changes. <UI>Put back the paste</UI> puts the reply back as you pasted it.

## Use a new key or remove it

<Shot id="jev-on" alt="The Jev Mode card with Jev Mode on: the line Your key ends in c0de, the Use a new key and Remove the key links, and the Turn off Jev Mode button" />

With a key saved, the card says <UI>Jev Mode is on</UI> or <UI>Jev Mode is off</UI>, and <UI>Your key ends in \{hint\}.</UI>

- To use a new key, click <UI>Use a new key</UI>, paste it under <UI>A new key</UI>, and click <UI>Replace the key</UI>. Jev Mode stays on or off, as it was.
- To delete the key from your keychain, click <UI>Remove the key</UI>. That also turns Jev Mode off.

## Turn it off

Open <UI>Jev Mode</UI> and click <UI>Turn off Jev Mode</UI>. The app keeps your key, so turning it back on is one click on <UI>Turn on Jev Mode</UI>. To delete the key as well, use <UI>Remove the key</UI>.

## When Jev can't answer

The judge card says why in one line, such as when TypeSafe refuses the key or Jev has had too many asks. A key problem points you back to <UI>Jev Mode</UI> in the top bar. Either way, you can still record your verdict. [When something breaks](/help/when-something-breaks/#jev-mode) lists each line and what to do.
