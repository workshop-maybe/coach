This page follows one bake-off from <UI>Compare with Jev</UI> to a changed verdict. Every request, grid and file below is built by the app's own code from this scenario, each time the guide is built. Only Jev's answers are made up: the guide never asks TypeSafe, so its numbers are illustrative. [Jev in compare, end to end](/jev/compare/) states each rule in full.

## The scenario

The badge is *Check an answer key*. Its task gives five quiz items, each with an answer key and a rationale, and asks whether each key holds. Its rubric has five points, one per item.

Claude Code and Codex each answered the badge's handoff, and you held both replies to compare them. Claude Code's reply gets all five calls right. Codex's calls the right key on item 4 wrong: it says the slice keeps the first item. Jev already read both replies in an earlier compare, and both readings meet the bar overall, Claude Code's at 90% and Codex's at 78%.

## The steps in the app

1. On home, under <UI>What needs you</UI>, click <UI>Compare \{n\} replies</UI> for *Check an answer key*. The compare opens with both replies, and the grid above them shows Jev's readings. With Jev Mode on, <UI>Compare with Jev</UI> sits above the grid, with a line saying what it sends.

<Shot id="compare-ask" alt="The bake-off on Check an answer key with Jev Mode on: Compare with Jev and the line Sends 1 request: 1 head-to-head above the grid of both replies' calls, with the two replies open below" />

2. Click <UI>Compare with Jev</UI>. While it asks, the link reads <UI>Asking Jev, \{k\} of \{n\}…</UI>. When the answer is saved, Claude Code's cells for points 3 and 4 say <UI>Better than \{names\}</UI>: Better than Codex.

<Shot id="compare-grid" alt="Claude Code's and Codex's held replies on Check an answer key after Compare with Jev: each column's calls and chances, and Better than Codex in Claude Code's cells for points 3 and 4" />

3. Click <UI>Met</UI> under Claude Code's reply. Codex's reply meets the bar in Jev's reading too, and on a quick read it looks right, so click <UI>Met</UI> under it as well. Each verdict agrees with its reply's reading, so each saves at once.

Had you clicked <UI>Not yet</UI> under Codex's reply while it was still held, a step would have opened under it before anything saved, since Not yet goes against Jev's meets:

<Shot id="compare-against-jev" alt="After Compare with Jev, Not yet pressed under Codex's held reply, which Jev called meets: Which points did it miss under the reply, with the five rubric points as buttons, none pressed, Jev's call beside each, and Back and Skip" />

4. Back in the grid, the mark on point 4 sends you to Codex's reply: its item 4 calls a right key wrong. Under it, click <UI>Change to Not yet</UI>. That goes against Jev's meets, so the same step opens: <UI>Which points did it miss</UI>, with Jev's call beside each point and none pressed.

<Shot id="compare-rejudge" alt="Both replies judged Met, and under Codex's reply after Change to Not yet: Which points did it miss, with the rubric points as buttons, Meets beside the first two, and Back and Skip" />

5. Tap the point for item 4, which Jev's reading can't call, and click <UI>Next</UI>. The step asks <UI>What did Jev miss</UI>. Write a line and click <UI>Save</UI>.

<Shot id="compare-reason" alt="What did Jev miss under Codex's reply: the line Jev called it meets. You said Not yet., the reason Calls the right key on item 4 wrong. typed in the box, and Back and Save" />

6. Codex's reply now says Not met, with <UI>Changed from Met</UI> at its foot and <UI>Change to Met</UI> beside it.

<Shot id="compare-changed" alt="The compare after Save: Claude Code's reply Met, and Codex's reply Not met with Change to Met and Changed from Met at its foot" />

## The marks

Each point's two answers are read against the fixed band. A reply covers a point better only when its own question is above 70% and the reverse below 30%. That holds on points 3 and 4, both for Claude Code. On the other points at least one answer is between 30% and 70%, so nothing is marked:

## The verdicts

Each verdict is read against its reply's own reading. Met under each reply agrees with its meets, so neither opens the step. Change to Not yet under Codex's goes against it, so the step opens. The head-to-head plays no part in this: only the reply's own reading can make a verdict go against Jev.

## What the change saves

Change to Not yet never rewrites the result. It writes a re-judge, a small file of its own with the new verdict, the point you tapped and your reason:

<Example file="compare/rejudge.md" caption="The re-judge file" wrap />

When the folder loads, the newest re-judge is laid over its result. The reply keeps its name, its time and its reading, and takes the new verdict, missed points and note. The verdict it had before goes on a list of earlier verdicts, which is where <UI>Changed from Met</UI> comes from:

<Example file="compare/rejudged-result.json" caption="Codex's result as the folder reads it after the change" />

<Source file="src/domain/folder.ts" symbol="layRejudges" />

The next second opinion on this badge counts Codex's reply once, as Not met. Its note makes it an overrule with a reason, so it ranks among the first examples, on the no side of point 4's question.
