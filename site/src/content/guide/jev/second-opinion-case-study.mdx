This page follows one second opinion from the click to the saved result. Every request, reading and file below is built by the app's own code from this scenario, each time the guide is built. Only Jev's answers are made up: the guide never asks TypeSafe, so its numbers are illustrative. [The second opinion, end to end](/jev/second-opinion/) states each rule in full.

## The scenario

The badge is *Check an answer key*, one of the app's presets. Its task gives five quiz items, each with an answer key and a rationale, and asks whether each key holds. Its rubric has five points, one per item, and one note: "Met when all five calls are right and each wrong key gets the right answer and the step that fails. Calling a right key wrong fails the test." It has two learning targets.

The folder already holds nine judged results on this badge from Claude Code and Codex, each saved with a reading from `jev-1.13.0`. Two of them carry notes, written when the verdict went against Jev's call.

Claude Code takes the test again and sends back this reply. It gets four items right, but it lets key 2 stand: 2100 is not a leap year.

## The steps in the app

1. Turn on Jev Mode from <UI>Jev Mode</UI> in the top bar, on home, as [Jev Mode](/help/jev-mode/#turn-it-on) shows.
2. Open *Check an answer key* and click <UI>Test it on an agent</UI>. Under <UI>Who takes it</UI>, click Claude Code.
3. Under <UI>Hand it over</UI>, click <UI>Copy</UI> and give the handoff to Claude Code. Paste its reply under <UI>What came back</UI> and click <UI>Next</UI>.
4. On <UI>Did it meet it</UI>, read the reply against the rubric and click <UI>Not yet</UI>. With Jev Mode on, <UI>Not yet</UI> is a choice, and nothing saves until you click <UI>Next</UI>. Under the rubric, <UI>Check with Jev</UI> appears, with a line saying four earlier replies go with it, and your notes.

<Shot id="case-check" alt="The rubric side of Did it meet it on Check an answer key, after Not yet: the five rubric points, Check with Jev under them, and the line Also sends 4 earlier replies on this badge with your notes" />

5. Click <UI>Check with Jev</UI>. While it asks, the link reads <UI>Asking Jev…</UI>. Jev's reading appears under the rubric: a call beside each point, the overall call and the learning targets. Since you've picked a verdict, the reading also shows Jev's track record on this badge, and that its cut-offs were fitted to your verdicts.

<Shot id="case-reading" alt="The foot of Jev's reading on Check an answer key: Meets on the last two points, Meets overall at 77%, one learning target Meets at 80% and one Can't call at 60%, then Jev matched your verdict 7 of 8 times on this badge, and couldn't call 1, and Cut-offs fitted to your verdicts" />

6. Click <UI>Next</UI>. The test asks <UI>Which points did it miss</UI>, with Jev's call beside each point and none pressed. Tap the second point, the leap-year key, which Jev calls <UI>Misses</UI>.

<Shot id="case-missed" alt="Which points did it miss: the five rubric points as buttons, none pressed, with Meets beside four of them and Misses beside the leap-year point, and Skip" />

7. Click <UI>Next</UI>. Jev called the reply meets overall and you said Not yet, so one more step asks <UI>What did Jev miss</UI>. Write a line and click <UI>Save</UI>.

<Shot id="case-reason" alt="What did Jev miss: the line Jev called it meets. You said Not yet., the reason Lets key 2 stand, but 2100 is not a leap year. typed in the box, and Save" />

The card then says <UI>\{agent\} does not hold it</UI>. The result is in your folder, with Jev's reading folded under the reply wherever it opens, as in this earlier result:

<Shot id="jev-reading" alt="Codex's reply on Check an answer key, with Jev's reading open below it: each rubric point called Meets with its chance beside it, Meets overall at 91%, and the two learning targets, one Meets and one Can't call" />

## Choosing the examples

When the judge card opens, the app ranks the badge's results as example sources. The two results with notes come first, newest first, then the rest, newest first. Here is the ranking, with which results the request finally quotes:

<Example file="second-opinion/ranking.json" caption="The badge's results as example sources, best first" />

Each side of each question takes its 2 best results:

- **The overall question** takes the two best Met results on its yes side, Codex's short reply from September 21 and its reply from September 27, and the two best Not met results on its no side, from September 15 and September 20.
- **The point questions** take the two best Met results on their yes side. Their no side stays empty: none of these results recorded missed points, so none says which point it missed.

That is four results, which is the count under <UI>Check with Jev</UI>. The ranking reads every result once per folder snapshot; each reply then walks the ranking and excerpts only the results some side still has room for.

## The ask

<UI>Check with Jev</UI> sends the fitted request through the Jev port. The answer lands only if nothing has moved since: the same test, the same reply, and no newer ask. The app freezes the reading with the model that answered and that model's cut-offs on the badge.
