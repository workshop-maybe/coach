A credential is a skill you can check: a name, learning targets, a task and a rubric. The app shows each one as a badge. [Credentials and badges](/ideas/credentials/) explains the parts.

On home, click <UI>Create a credential</UI>. The first card asks <UI>Where to start</UI>.

<Shot id="credential-start" alt="Where to start: Build your own, then six presets such as Name what matters next, Say how sure you are and Check an answer key" />

## Start from a preset

A preset comes with its learning targets, task and rubric already written, such as *Say how sure you are* or *Check an answer key*.

1. Click a preset. Its card lists its learning targets.
2. Click <UI>Make the badge</UI>.

## Build your own

Pick something you know well enough to judge, such as planning a week of dinners.

1. Click <UI>Build your own</UI>.
2. Under <UI>What is it called</UI>, type a name and click <UI>Next</UI>.
3. Under <UI>One thing its holder can do</UI>, write one learning target, starting "I can". Click <UI>Next</UI>.
4. Under <UI>The task</UI>, write what the agent is asked to do, where, and what it hands back.
5. Click <UI>Make the badge</UI>.

Stuck on the task? <UI>Suggest a task</UI> copies a prompt you can paste into your assistant. It asks for two or three tasks that would test your target, each with a rubric.

Nothing is saved until you click <UI>Make the badge</UI>, and closing the card throws your answers away. Once you've written a task, Escape steps back a card instead of closing, so a stray key can't lose it.

## Keep a target to the skill

The app checks each learning target as you type it. A target that also says how you'll check it, with a list, a number or a "by doing this" clause, gets <UI>Move to the rubric</UI> beside the part it would move.

<Shot id="credential-target" alt="One thing its holder can do, with the target I can plan a week of dinners by checking what's already in the fridge, and Move to the rubric beside By checking what's already in the fridge" />

Click <UI>Move to the rubric</UI> to cut that part from the target. The app keeps it as the first line of the badge's rubric. A target that joins two skills gets the question <UI>Two skills, or one workflow?</UI> Neither one stops you.

## After you make it

The new badge shows its art and <UI>Test it on an agent</UI>. It is also on home, under <UI>Badges</UI>.

A badge you build yourself starts with one learning target and no rubric, apart from anything you moved there. Add the rest before its first test: open it from home and click <UI>Learning targets</UI> or <UI>Rubric</UI>. [Change a badge](/how-to/change-a-badge/) shows how.

When you're ready, [hand off a test](/how-to/hand-off-a-test/).
